Social Engineering: The Human Hack

Social engineering exploits human trust. Instead of hacking systems, attackers hack people. Here are some real-life traps to test yourself 👇

Scenario 1: “IT Support” Calls You

Attacker: Hi, this is IT support. We noticed login issues on your account. Can you share your employee ID and reset code?
You: Okay, let me check...

Scenario 2: Fake Bank Alert Email

From: bank-security@secureupdate.com

We detected unusual activity. Click below to verify.

secure-bank-update.com

Scenario 3: “I sent the code to you by mistake!”

Attacker: Brooo I mistakenly sent my Facebook recovery code to your number. Please send it to me!
You: Wait what?

Scenario 4: “You won an iPhone 15”

Congratulations! You won a free iPhone.

free-iphone-gift-event.net

Scenario 5: Fake Facebook Security Update

Your account was restricted. Log in to verify.

meta-security-check.com

Scenario 6: Delivery Scam “Small Fee Required”

Your shipment is waiting. Pay $3.50 to release the package.

post-service-fee-pay.com

Scenario 7: PayPal / Stripe Verification Scam

Your PayPal is limited. Verify your identity now:

paypal-verify-center.com

Scenario 8: USB Drive on the Floor (Baiting)

You find a USB labeled “Salary Data 2025”. You plug it in.

Scenario 9: Fake Survey Reward

Fill this survey and win a $100 gift card.

survey-reward-giftcard.net

Scenario 10: CEO Fraud (Urgent Payment Request)

CEO: I need you to wire $5,000 ASAP. No time to ask questions!
You: Should I confirm with finance?

Scenario 11: Free Public Wi-Fi

You connect to “FREE-Airport-WiFi”. It’s actually malicious.

Scenario 12: Fake QR Code

You scan a QR code at a café. It leads to a login page.

Scenario 13: Fake Job Interview

You are asked to install a “meeting tool” that is malware.

Scenario 14: Fake Internship Offer

You are asked to pay “registration fees”.

Scenario 15: Fake Company Invoice

A supplier sends you a PDF with a changed bank account.

🧠 Key Social Engineering Techniques

  • Phishing: Fake emails or messages.
  • Pretexting: Pretending to be someone legitimate.
  • Baiting: Using infected USB drives.
  • Tailgating: Entering restricted areas behind others.
  • Vishing: Voice phishing calls.
